# When the user wants photorealism

## Why not

This method generates still assets and assembles them drawing by drawing in code. In a photoreal picture viewers expect smooth 24 fps motion, true 3D turns, real light and fluids; an assembled version gives itself away: deforming turns, subjects frozen in slow motion, pasted-on sticker look. In stop-motion, on twos and pose-by-pose changes are the medium's own character, so lemo-animi only does stop-motion styles.

## How to steer

Don't just refuse. Give the reason in one sentence, then offer 2–3 options from the fixed styles, each with one sentence describing the picture, in the user's language. Example — the user asks for "a horse galloping across the grassland at full speed, photoreal":

> A photoreal galloping horse would fall apart with this method (the leg motion isn't fluid enough and turns deform). Options:
> ① Wooden puppet stop-motion: a carved little horse runs across a wooden grassland, joints moving in small jerks like an old toy;
> ② Needle-felt stop-motion: a fuzzy felt pony runs over felt grass, its mane flicking in tufts;
> ③ Layered paper: a paper-cut horse silhouette runs past layer after layer of paper grass, backlit and glowing.
> Which one?

Recommend only styles listed by `animi.py styles`. Toy-like stop-motion (wooden puppet, tin, clay) comes closest to "a real thing moving" — list those first.

If the user insists on realism: say this skill can't do it; don't force it and don't spend image-generation credits.
